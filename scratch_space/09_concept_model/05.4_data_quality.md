## what this sub-plan is

[`05.2_perf_followups.md`](05.2_perf_followups.md), extending to the five-language

## emerging direction (decisions)

The first full build raised three strategic questions, now resolved (2026-06-18):
**(1) drop kaikki**, **(2) no hard frequency cap** (frequency is a priority signal, not a
boundary), and **(3)** the licensing posture follows from (1). Recorded here and
cross-linked into [`00-concepts-brainstorm.md`](00-concepts-brainstorm.md) (the canonical
decision doc) and phase 6.

### 1. Drop kaikki - decided (drop it)

kaikki was adopted mainly because a wiktextract loader existed from an older project, not
because it was the best fit. The first build shows the cost: the sense-blind `(text,
language)` join produces the `house` mess (7,220 `definition == lemma` rows; non-en
glosses that are bare English words, `PSEUDOGAP!` junk, or form-of notes - see
[findings](#findings---first-full-build-2026-06-18-enptesfrit)), and it drags in
CC-BY-SA over ~53% of concept glosses.

**Decision: drop kaikki.** kaikki entries are lemma-only (no concept id), so they never
contributed to the part we actually care about, and their text is the low-quality,
viral-licensed part. What we need from the dataset is narrow:

- **a good English gloss** - already covered by OMW (en gloss coverage is ~100%), with
  the CILI/ILI English gloss as a permissive fallback for the few gaps; and
- **good concept grouping** - the cross-lingual lemma sets per concept, which come from
  OMW's ILI grouping, **not** from kaikki.

Per-language (non-English) glosses are **not a priority**, so kaikki's main "value" (the
es/pt/fr/it gloss top-up) is something we do not need - dropping it costs nothing we care
about and is *not* "ship sparser" for the English-gloss-plus-grouping target. No LLM
target-language gloss backfill is required for the core (it can be added later, per
language, if a consumer ever needs it). Examples, if wanted, come from **Tatoeba**
(CC-BY, attribution only), not kaikki. This removes the junk *and* the only viral license
in one move. See [`metadata_catalog.md`](05.4_data_quality/metadata_catalog.md) and
[`other_datasets.md`](05.4_data_quality/other_datasets.md).

### 2. Scope: a learner lexicon - frequency is a signal, not a hard cap

The target is **support for a language-learning app**, not the end-all corpus, and the
first build is large (**147k en lemmas / 117k concepts**, most of them rare). But the
decision is **no hard frequency cap**: a low-frequency lemma is *kept* when it is
**well-connected in the graph** (rich concept membership / relations) or
**pedagogically important** (e.g. irregular verbs), which a blunt top-N rank would wrongly
drop. So:

- **Frequency is a priority/ranking signal**, used to order enrichment and to surface a
  learner-facing core - **not** a gate that deletes rows.
- **Long-tail pruning is an optional cleanup pass**, applied only if a *specific* junk
  pattern shows up (e.g. the multiword/digit/`PSEUDOGAP`-style noise), not a blanket cut.
- Connectivity and learning-value override raw frequency when deciding what to keep.

This keeps phase 6 as **frequency/CEFR annotation + prioritization**, not a corpus-boundary
filter. (The memory ceiling from 5.1/5.3 is then handled by the 5.3 streaming/cache work
and, if needed, a targeted long-tail cleanup - not by capping the lexicon.) Recorded into
[`06_frequency_complexity.md`](06_frequency_complexity.md).

### 3. What the licensing tangle actually means (overlapping licenses)

"Open" splits into three buckets that combine very differently:

- **Public domain / CC0** (Wikidata Lexemes): no conditions; combine freely.
- **Permissive / attribution** (Apache-2.0, MIT, CC-BY, most OMW lexicons): combine
  freely, just preserve attribution/notices. **Non-viral** - does not constrain the rest.
- **Copyleft / share-alike** (CC-BY-SA: Wiktionary/kaikki, ConceptNet; LGPL/GPL on some
  word lists): **viral**. If the dataset incorporates or derives from CC-BY-SA *text*,
  the combined/derivative work must itself be offered under CC-BY-SA. You cannot relicense
  that part to CC-BY or CC0.

What "overlapping" does in practice:

- **Facts vs expression.** Raw facts (a lemma-concept edge, a frequency number) are
  generally not copyrightable; the specific *text* of a gloss/example is. Our
  concept/lemma/sense **edges** are low-risk; the license rides on the `definitions` /
  `examples` **strings**, tracked per row by the `source` column.
- **One SA source caps the whole shipped corpus.** A single CC-BY-SA field cannot be
  relicensed down, so mixing kaikki text into a shipped table pulls the dataset to
  CC-BY-SA - unless you keep the SA text in a *separate* artifact (legally weaker for
  data than for code: a single merged Parquet of mixed-license rows is hard to call
  "mere aggregation") or simply do not ship it.
- **Resolution.** If kaikki is the *only* share-alike source (it is, here), **dropping it
  (1) removes the only viral constraint**, and the dataset ships cleanly as permissive
  (OMW) + CC0 (Wikidata) + CC-BY (Tatoeba) with an attribution-only dataset card. That is
  the cleanest open posture and the concrete reason (1) and (3) are the same decision.

**"So if we keep a viral source, the whole corpus is just CC-BY-SA - and we'd be ok?"**
Yes, mostly. CC-BY-SA *is* a legitimate free/open license, so shipping the whole dataset
under it is a valid option (brainstorm option (b)). The cost is **downstream**: share-alike
is viral, so anyone who redistributes a modified version of the corpus (and arguably the
app, if it bundles a derived copy of the data) must also license that under CC-BY-SA, and
it can't ever be relicensed to a more permissive form - it forecloses CC-BY/CC0/proprietary
reuse forever. (Using the data inside an app generally does not make the *app code*
CC-BY-SA; redistributing the *data* is what carries the obligation - the data/software
distinction here is legally fuzzy, which is itself a reason to avoid the question.) Given
decision (1) - we do not need kaikki's text at all (English gloss + grouping come from OMW)
- there is **no reason to accept CC-BY-SA**: staying permissive/CC0/CC-BY keeps every
downstream option open at zero cost. So "we'd be ok" is true but strictly worse than just
dropping kaikki.

Full per-source table in
[`other_datasets.md`](05.4_data_quality/other_datasets.md#summary-licensing-posture-feeds-phase-10);
final license mechanics are phase 10.

## datasets overview

The first build has exactly the two sources the phase-5 design picked, joined by the
phase-5 transform. Nothing else is in the data yet (frequency, CEFR, relations are all
later phases). Full field inventory in
[`metadata_catalog.md`](05.4_data_quality/metadata_catalog.md); the short version:

| Source | Role in the build | Native unit | What we currently keep | License |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **OMW 1.4** via `wn` 1.1.0 | concept **backbone** (every row originates here) | synset (a cross-lingual meaning, linked by ILI) | synset id, ILI key, per-language gloss, member lemmas, POS code | per-wordnet; OMW core is permissive (the brainstorm's Apache-2.0 claim is **per-lexicon, verify** - see catalog) |
| **kaikki.org** (wiktextract) | **enrichment** only (fills sparse glosses + adds examples; never adds rows) | Wiktionary headword (lemma-centric, no concept id) | first gloss per missing language, example sentences | CC-BY-SA (share-alike, license-isolated) |

Key consequences that drive the checks:

- **OMW decides the shape; kaikki only fills holes.** A concept exists because an OMW
  synset exists; its definitions start as OMW glosses and only get a kaikki gloss for a
  language OMW left blank ([`transform._enrich_concepts`](../../src/lang_tools/lexicon/ingestion/transform.py)).
  So gloss coverage is really "OMW coverage per language, topped up by whatever kaikki
  headword happened to match." That top-up join is `(normalized text, language)` only -
  no sense alignment - which is the root of the `house` problem (see
  [`models_explained.md`](05.4_data_quality/models_explained.md)).
- **Cross-lingual grouping is ILI-driven.** Two languages share a `Concept` only when
  their synsets share an ILI. Synsets with a falsy ILI fall back to grouping by
  `synset_id` alone and stay **monolingual** ([`omw._grouping_key`](../../src/lang_tools/lexicon/ingestion/sources/omw.py)).
  How many concepts are ILI-backed vs ILI-orphaned is a first-class metric (it bounds
  how much cross-lingual linking we actually got).
- **POS comes only from OMW** in the concept path (`synset.pos`, mapped by
  `_POS_LABELS`); the kaikki POS is parsed (`WikiRecord.pos`) but **not** carried onto
  the OMW-derived lemma. So "can we trust POS" is really "do we trust the OMW synset
  POS", and the two sources disagreeing is itself a signal worth measuring.
- **Provenance is per row.** Each lemma/concept/sense carries an `omw|kaikki|llm|manual`
  tag (`codec.PROVENANCE_COL`, dropped on model load). The kaikki-tagged share is the
  CC-BY-SA-touched surface and the phase-10 attribution input - report it.

## planned preliminary checks

Grouped so each group maps to one notebook section under `05.4_data_quality/`. Use
whichever access path is simplest per check: **DuckDB point/filter queries over the
Parquet** (`inspect_table`) for the big aggregate counts, **or a full store load** when
that makes a check easier - **both are fine now**. The phase-5.3 fix removed the old
blocker: streaming load + a persisted signature-keyed `_store.sqlite` mean cold load is
~16 s / 593 MB for en/pt (warm cache ~1 ms), with the five-language build projected
~1.2-1.5 GB ([`05.3`](05.3_load_profiling.md)). On this **32 GB box** that is
comfortable, so we can push it - the >5 min/OOM Observation 2 in
[`05.2`](05.2_perf_followups.md) saw was swap thrash on a small machine, since fixed.
The only thing to still avoid is naive repeated `get_all_*` full-table reconstruction
in a loop; a single up-front load (warm-cached) is cheap.

### 1. Volumes (the "how many" table)

Per table and per language: counts of `concepts`, `lemmas`, `senses`, plus
`definitions` (gloss key/value pairs) and `examples`. Cross-checks:

- `senses == sum over concepts of members == sum over lemmas of memberships`
  (the edge is the only membership record - it must reconcile both ways).
- lemmas-per-concept and concepts-per-lemma distributions (min/median/p95/max).
- a `(language, POS)` matrix.

### 2. Emptiness / degenerate cardinality (the draft's core ask)

- **Empty concepts**: no definitions in *any* language; and the weaker "no definition
  in language L". Expect a long tail for non-en languages (OMW gloss sparsity).
- **Empty / single-member concepts**: `senses == 0` (orphan concept) and `senses == 1`
  (no synonym, no cross-lingual partner - a "concept" that is really one word).
- **Single-language concepts**: definitions/members in exactly one language = the
  ILI-orphan or unmatched set; quantify vs multi-language concepts (the cross-lingual
  win the whole design is for).
- **Lemmas with no sense** (should be zero - every lemma is created with an edge; a
  nonzero count means a transform bug).
- **Single-gloss / single-example**: concepts with exactly one definition; lemmas with
  exactly one (or zero) example. Examples are expected to be **very** sparse (only
  kaikki adds them, only on a matched headword).
- **Senses are text-free** so "empty sense" is not meaningful; check instead for
  **dangling edges**: a `sense` whose `lemma_id` or `concept_id` has no row.

### 3. Cross-lingual balance (the "imbalances between languages" ask)

- Per-language: # lemmas, # concepts touched, # glosses, # examples, gloss-coverage
  ratio (glosses / concepts-touched). English will dominate (OMW is en-centric and
  `_pick_slug_source` prefers en); quantify the skew so phase 6/8 know where enrichment
  is most needed.
- **ILI linkage**: share of concepts that are ILI-backed (multi-language-capable) vs
  synset-id-only (monolingual by construction).
- **kaikki match rate per language**: of each language's lemmas, how many found a
  kaikki entry (= got a gloss top-up or examples). This is the enrichment yield and
  varies a lot by language dump quality.

### 4. Trust / dedup signals (the "can we trust POS / what helps a cleanup pass" ask)

- **POS sanity**: distribution of mapped POS; count synsets with a missing/odd `pos`
  code; where a lemma also exists as a kaikki headword, **OMW-POS vs kaikki-POS
  agreement rate** (disagreement = a review queue, not an auto-fix).
- **Slug collisions** (Observation 1, [`05.2`](05.2_perf_followups.md)): per slug, how
  many distinct concept ids share it; top offenders; share of concepts whose slug is
  `"concept"` or a bare English lemma. Pure legibility (the `hash[:12]` keeps ids
  unique), routed to phase 8 - but **quantified here**.
- **Definition = lemma** (the `house` smell): count concepts where a per-language
  definition string, normalized, equals one of that concept's lemma forms (a gloss that
  is just the word, carrying no meaning). Break down by source tag (is it an OMW gloss
  or a kaikki top-up?).
- **Near-duplicate lemmas**: same `normalized` differing only by accent/case within a
  language (the `Lemma.id` already dedups exact normalized matches; this finds the
  near-misses a cleanup pass might merge).
- **Suspicious lemmas**: multi-word members, members containing digits/punctuation,
  very long forms - candidates to drop or flag before shipping.

### 5. Provenance & licensing snapshot (feeds phase 10)

- Row counts by `source` tag per table; the **kaikki-tagged share** (CC-BY-SA surface).
- Confirm `senses` are 100% `omw` (they carry no text, so by policy they never flip to
  `kaikki`); a `kaikki` sense would be a transform bug.

### Output

A single short report (printed in the notebook + a committed `report.md` under
`05.4_data_quality/`) with the count tables and the top-N lists for collisions /
definition-equals-lemma / POS disagreements. No data is modified. Each finding links to
the phase that fixes it. Memory headroom is fine on the 32 GB box (Observation 3's
borderline memory was the small machine, resolved by the 5.3 streaming/cache fix), so
the notebook can load the full store when convenient; just keep the top-N lists bounded
so the printed report stays readable.

## findings - first full build (2026-06-18, en/pt/es/fr/it)

Numbers from the auto-generated [`05.4_data_quality/report.md`](05.4_data_quality/report.md)
(regenerated by the notebook). Headlines:

- **Volumes**: 117,659 concepts / 321,126 lemmas / 491,876 senses. English dominates
  (147k lemmas vs 36-51k each for the others) - expected, OMW is en-centric.
- **Reconciliation is clean**: zero dangling edges and zero lemmas-without-a-sense. No
  transform bug; the build is referentially sound.
- **Cross-lingual grouping works**: 70.4% of concepts are multi-language (the design's
  whole point); 30% are single-language (the ILI-orphan / unmatched tail).
- **Gloss coverage falls off sharply outside English**: en 100%, it 87.5%, fr 76.7%,
  pt 71.5%, **es 70.1%**. The 30% gap for es/pt is the main phase-8 enrichment target.
  Also 54,769 concepts (~47%) carry a single gloss only.
- **Examples are very sparse**: 81% of lemmas have none (only kaikki adds them); kaikki
  match rate is en 34.9% but just 4-8% for the other four - low enrichment yield.
- **POS is fully mapped** (no `<null>` bucket), so the OMW->label map covers the data;
  the build is noun-heavy (256k of 321k).
- **Slug collisions are large but benign**: 117,659 concepts share only 86,384 distinct
  slugs (~27% collide), led by common verbs (`cut` 54, `break` 50, `run` 35); the
  generic `concept` slug appears once. Legibility only (ids unique via hash) -> phase 8.
- **`definition == lemma` (the `house` defect) quantified**: 7,220 rows across 6,568
  concepts (~5.6%) where a language's definition is just one of its own lemma forms.
- **Provenance / licensing**: senses are 100% `omw` (correct). But kaikki touches a
  large surface - **62,612 of 117,659 concept glosses (~53%)** and 62,364 lemmas
  (~19%) are `kaikki`-tagged. That CC-BY-SA share is bigger than "enrichment only"
  implies and is a real phase-10 license input.

**New defect the data surfaced (not in the original spec): kaikki glosses land under
the wrong language and are often not target-language text.** The `house` spot-check
shows the `family` concept with `es: "house"` / `pt: "house"` (bare English word), plus
`it: "PSEUDOGAP!"` (wiktextract junk) and `pt: "plural of empresa"` / `pt: "to host (an
event)"` (English-language *form-of* glosses stored under a non-en key). So beyond
`definition == lemma`, many non-en `definitions` values are **English text or
metadata**, not a definition in that language. This is the sense-blind `(text,
language)` join picking a kaikki headword's first/most-common gloss regardless of
language or sense. Worth a dedicated follow-up check (definition-language detection:
flag a `definitions[lang]` whose detected language != `lang`) and strongly motivates
the phase-5 optional LLM mapping / phase-8 enrichment. Routed to **phase 8**.

## routing of findings (who fixes what)

- gloss sparsity, `definition == lemma`, slug collisions, orphan/empty concepts,
  POS-disagreement review -> **phase 8** (LLM maintenance; it already lists slug-dedup
  and gloss enrichment).
- missing frequency / CEFR (everything is null today) -> **phase 6**.
- missing relations (hypernymy/antonymy all empty) -> **phase 7**.
- per-source license tallies + attribution -> **phase 10**.
- any reconciliation failure (dangling edge, lemma-without-sense, kaikki-tagged sense)
  -> a **transform bug**, back to phase 5 - these are the only "must fix now" outcomes.
