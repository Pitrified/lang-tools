# Licensing - start

## Original note

Licensing was phase 10 of [`09_concept_model`](../09_concept_model/10_licensing_docs.md),
the last open phase of that folder.
It moved here on 2026-11-12, after its distribution part went to
[`17_distribution`](../17_distribution/00_start.md), so that 09 can close.
The goal stated there: the corpus ships as a free and open dataset, with every source credited.

Why it is not a write-up: the July rebuild found that the "no share-alike anywhere" posture of
the 5.5 cleanup does not hold
([`05.56_rebuild_gate.md`](../09_concept_model/05.56_rebuild_gate/05.56_rebuild_gate.md),
Stage C), and the decision was left to this phase.

## Current state

What the build writes into the corpus (checked on the 2026-11-11 build, `data/lexicon/`):

- **Senses by source:** 490,945 `omw`, 49,675 `inflection`, 780 `manual`.
- **Backbone:** the five OMW 1.4 lexicons, read through `wn` (`sources/omw.py` `OMW_LEXICONS`).
- **English gloss fallback:** CILI (`sources/cili.py`).
- **Frequency and CEFR bands:** computed from `wordfreq` 3.1.1 (`_build.json` `enrichment`).
- **Our own text, re-applied from `data/curated/`:** reviewed or LLM-written glosses, gloss
  backfills, slug qualifiers, look-alike labels and notes, sense additions and drops, best-sense
  pins, example sentences.
- **Inflected forms:** generated by our own rules (`ingestion/inflections.py`). kaikki is used only
  in the scratchpad, to measure the rules (`15_inflection_model/00_start.md` Q1).

Not in the corpus, so out of this folder:
Tatoeba and Wikidata (staged, not built in), the CEFR graded lists (validation only), kaikki.

Already published: the bootstrap seed (`data/bootstrap/`) and the curated inputs are committed to
the GitHub repo through LFS. The seed holds OMW rows, pt included.

The repo has no `LICENSE` file and no `license` field in `pyproject.toml`.

## Sources and their licenses

Checked on 2026-11-12, from the cached `wn` lexicon metadata and the upstream pages.

| Source | Used for | License | Terms |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| omw-en 1.4 (Princeton WordNet 3.0) | en backbone | WordNet license | permissive, keep the notice |
| omw-pt 1.4 (OpenWN-PT) | pt backbone | CC BY-SA (no version in the metadata) | **share-alike** |
| omw-es 1.4 (MCR Spanish) | es backbone | CC BY 3.0 | attribution |
| omw-fr 1.4 (WOLF) | fr backbone | CeCILL-C v1 | **weak copyleft**, a software license |
| omw-it 1.4 (MultiWordNet) | it backbone | CC BY 3.0 | attribution |
| CILI | en gloss fallback | CC BY 4.0 | attribution |
| wordfreq 3.1.1 | frequency, CEFR bands | code Apache-2.0; data CC BY-SA 4.0 | **share-alike** on the data |
| OpenAI model output | curated glosses, notes, sentences | the terms give the output to the user | ours to license |

Notes on the rows:

- **OpenWordnet-PT upstream** (github.com/own-pt/openWordnet-PT) is CC BY 4.0 per its README and
  `LICENSE`, and the `wn` index has it as a separate project, `own-pt` 1.0.0
  (`own-pt.tar.gz` from the upstream release, WN-LMF "compatible with WN Python Library").
  A later upstream release, v1.1.0, is built with Cygnet. Its coverage and ILI mapping against
  omw-pt 1.4 are unmeasured.
- **WOLF** has no newer license found; CeCILL-C since the 2008 release. It is a French software
  license (LGPL-compatible); how it reads on data is unclear.
- **wordfreq** names its data sources: Wikipedia, OpenSubtitles 2018, SUBTLEX, NewsCrawl,
  GlobalVoices, Google Books Ngrams, OSCAR, Twitter, Reddit, plus the Leeds Internet Corpus and
  ParaCrawl. SUBTLEX asks derived works to credit its authors; OpenSubtitles asks for attribution.
  We ship numbers derived from it (zipf values and bands), not its word lists as such.
- **OpenAI output:** the terms assign whatever rights OpenAI has in the output to the user.
  Whether that output is copyrightable at all is a separate question; either way nothing stops
  us licensing it.

The facts-versus-expression line from `09_concept_model/05.4_data_quality.md` section 3 and
`15_inflection_model/00_start.md` still applies: edges and numbers are facts, while glosses and
examples carry expression. The EU database right protects extracting a substantial part of a
database apart from copyright, which is why "facts" does not end the question.

- Q9: Can someone else take the dataset and sell an app built on it, and can the user?
  The code license (MIT or Apache-2.0) does not decide this: the dataset has its own license.
  - Under CC BY 4.0 (Q4 a), anyone may sell an app built on the dataset, with attribution.
    The user may too: every shipped source allows commercial use (Princeton WordNet, own-pt,
    MCR, MultiWordNet and CILI are permissive or CC BY; the wordfreq-derived numbers are read as
    facts per Q3), and our own additions are the user's.
  - A licensor is never bound by their own license, so a stricter dataset license would limit
    only others. CC BY-NC 4.0 on the dataset would stop others selling it; it can cover only
    our additions (the reviewed glosses, pins, look-alikes, sense fixes, inflected forms), since
    the upstream wordnets stay available to anyone under their own terms. Those additions are
    most of the dataset's value for a learner app.
  - The choice is one-way. A CC license is irrevocable (CC BY 4.0 section 2(a)(1)): a release
    made under CC BY stays CC BY for anyone who has a copy, and they may sell it and pass it on
    under CC BY indefinitely. Switching to CC BY-NC later covers only the later releases.
    The other direction is free: a CC BY-NC release can be relaxed to CC BY at any time.
    The data already in the GitHub repo states no license today, so nothing has been granted yet.
  - a. Keep CC BY 4.0: anyone may sell, with attribution.
  - b. CC BY-NC 4.0 on the dataset: others may use it but not sell it; the user keeps every
    right, and can grant a commercial license case by case.
  - c. Both: CC BY-NC 4.0 public, a separate commercial license from the user on request.
  - Recommended: a, to keep the "free and open" goal: NC is not an open license under the usual
    definitions, its "commercial" line is unclear (an ad-supported app, a paid tutor), and it
    is hard to enforce on a solo project. Pick b if stopping a reseller matters more than open.
  - ANS: a (user, 2026-11-13): CC BY 4.0, free and open; anyone may use it, also to make
    money.

## Code license

The user's goal (Q5): package the database and use it in other projects, such as fala, an
Android app in Flutter, without closing a door.

- **fala takes the data, not the code.** fala is Dart; it would bundle the built database (a
  SQLite or Parquet file) as an app asset. What travels into fala is governed by the dataset
  license (Q4 a, CC BY 4.0) and the source licenses under it, not by the code license.
- **CC BY 4.0 fits an app bundle.** It allows any use, commercial or closed, with attribution:
  fala needs a licenses or about screen naming the dataset and the sources the card lists. The
  root license file (Q6 c) is written so fala can copy the text from it.
- **The code license never binds its owner.** As the copyright holder, the user can reuse
  lang-tools code anywhere whatever its license. A code license only says what other people
  may do, and what license their contributions come under.
- **What would close a door:** a copyleft code license (GPL, AGPL), because a contributor's
  code would then be usable only under it, including in the user's own closed projects. With
  no license at all, nobody else may use the code, and a contribution's terms are unclear.
- **MIT against Apache-2.0.** Both are permissive and allow anything. Apache-2.0 adds a patent
  grant and asks for a NOTICE file to be carried along; MIT is shorter and matches go-accenter,
  go-firefly and show-epub. For a solo project with no patents, MIT.

## Probe: own-pt 1.0.0 against omw-pt 1.4

Run on 2026-11-12, read-only: own-pt downloaded through the `wn` index into a scratchpad
database, compared with the cached omw-pt 1.4, `data/` untouched.
The comparison unit is the (word, ILI) pair, since the build groups concepts by ILI.
Words are compared as the wordnets spell them, before the build's spelling reform.

| | omw-pt 1.4 | own-pt 1.0.0 |
| --- | --- | --- |
| synsets, all with an ILI | 43,895 | 52,670 |
| distinct words | 54,071 | 56,032 |
| (word, ILI) pairs | 74,012 | 83,762 |
| definitions | 0 | 7,557 synsets (7,993 texts) |
| examples | 0 | 2,821 |

- **Mapping.** 43,862 of omw-pt's 43,895 ILIs are in own-pt; 33 are not, and own-pt adds 8,808.
  Pairs: 67,152 shared, 6,860 only in omw-pt, 16,610 only in own-pt.
- **Frequent words** (wordfreq top lists, as a word in the wordnet at all):
  top 1,000: omw-pt 669, own-pt 664 (27 lost, 22 gained);
  top 5,000: 2,790 against 2,781; top 20,000: 7,761 against 7,846.
  The 27 lost include function words the cards filter anyway (*de*, *do*, *no*, *se*, *ao*),
  plurals the inflection rules generate (*pessoas*, *países*, *armas*, *filmes*), and *é*, whose
  junk "letter" sense is an open point; and real losses such as *Brasil*, *sobre*, *há*, *qual*,
  *deve*, *enquanto*. A rebuild would show which of them come back through the other stages.
- **Definitions.** omw-pt has no pt definitions: every pt gloss in the corpus today is ours
  (43,927 pt rows in `gloss_backfill.jsonl`). own-pt's definitions include Wikipedia leads
  (91 with "(em francês: ...; ouça)"-style text, 407 over 200 characters), and Wikipedia text is
  CC BY-SA. So taking own-pt would mean its words and links only, keeping our glosses.
- **Curated rows.** Of 962 pt best-sense pins with a concept, 267 sit on an omw-pt pair and 8 of
  those lose the pair in own-pt. 53 of the 689 pt sense additions become moot (own-pt already has
  the pair). 155 of the 266 pt member-form drops still apply. 191 of the 8,440 pt look-alike words
  are in omw-pt and not in own-pt (228 are in neither).
- **Not measured:** own-pt's v1.1.0 release (built with Cygnet) is not in the `wn` index; the
  effect on cards needs a rebuild.
